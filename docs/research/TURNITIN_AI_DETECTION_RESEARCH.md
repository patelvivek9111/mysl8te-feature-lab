## Executive Summary

Turnitin's AI Writing Detection is a paid add-on feature that uses a proprietary machine learning model to identify text that may be generated by AI tools (ChatGPT, other LLMs), AI paraphrasers (Quillbot), or AI bypasser tools. It analyzes "qualifying text" (long-form prose) and provides a percentage score indicating the likelihood of AI generation. The system includes **critical limitations**: it can misidentify human-written, AI-generated, and paraphrased text, and **should NOT be used as sole evidence for academic misconduct**.

**Key Finding**: Turnitin explicitly disclaims that AI detection "may not always be accurate" and requires "further scrutiny and human judgment" before taking adverse action against students.

**Rejections**:
- Files below 300 words
- Files above 30,000 words
- Files in unsupported languages
- Files without qualifying text (see below)

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED

### 2.2 Qualifying Text Definition (DOCUMENTED)

**What IS Qualifying Text**:
- Prose sentences in paragraphs
- Long-form writing (essays, dissertations, articles)
- Academic writing style

**What is NOT Qualifying Text**:
- Poetry
- Scripts
- Code
- Bullet points
- Tables
- Annotated bibliographies
- Short-form writing
- Non-prose formats

**Impact**:
Documents mixing prose and non-prose will show disparity between percentage and highlights (percentage only applies to qualifying text).

## 4. Confidence Scoring and Thresholds

### 4.1 Percentage Reporting (DOCUMENTED)

**Source**: [AI Writing Indicator States](https://guides.ithenticate.com/hc/en-us/articles/27835303398541)

**Score Interpretation**:

1. **0% Detected**
   - No qualifying text identified as AI-generated
   - Model did not flag content

2. **1-19% Range → Displayed as `*%`** (Important Change)
   - Scores between 1% and 19% NOT shown numerically
   - Displayed as asterisk (`*%`) instead
   - **Reason**: Higher incidence of false positives in this range
   - No percentage value given to avoid misinterpretation
   - **Change date**: July 8, 2024
   - Pre-July 2024 reports may still show numeric scores <20%

3. **20-100% Range**
   - Numeric percentage displayed
   - Indicates proportion of qualifying text flagged as likely AI
   - Still requires human review

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED

### 4.2 False Positive Rates (DOCUMENTED)

**Source**: [Understanding False Positives](https://www.turnitin.com/blog/understanding-the-false-positive-rate-for-sentences-of-our-ai-writing-detection-capabilities)

**Official Metrics**:

1. **Document-Level False Positive Rate**
   - **Claimed**: Less than 1% for documents with 20%+ AI writing
   - Means: <1% chance human-written document scored as 20%+ AI

2. **Sentence-Level False Positive Rate**
   - **Measured**: Approximately 4%
   - Means: Individual highlighted sentences may be incorrectly flagged
   - **Implication**: Do NOT rely on sentence-level highlights alone

**Institutional Guidance** (DOCUMENTED):
Multiple universities (e.g., Vanderbilt, University of Melbourne) issued guidance:
- Focus only on scores >20%
- Use entire document, not sentence-by-sentence
- Be mindful of formulaic writing (may trigger false positives)
- Second piece of evidence required before alleging misconduct
- Never use AI score alone as evidence

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED (official metrics), DOCUMENTED (institutional concerns)

### 5.2 Model Limitations from Research (DOCUMENTED)

**Source**: Academic studies on Turnitin AI detection

**Temple University Study** (Evaluating Effectiveness):

**Test Methodology**:
- 30 human-written samples
- 30 AI-generated samples
- 30 "disguised" AI samples (paraphrased, modified)
- 30 hybrid samples (mixed human + AI)

**Results**:
1. **Human-written**: 93% correctly identified (28/30)
   - 1 false positive at 11% AI
   - 1 unable to be rated

2. **AI-generated**: 77% fully correct (23/30)
   - 5 rated as partially AI (52-97%)
   - 2 unable to be rated

3. **Disguised AI**: 63% fully correct (19/30)
   - 8 rated as partially AI (43-95%)
   - 3 unable to be rated
   - **Significant drop in accuracy**

4. **Hybrid**: Most challenging
   - Only 43% technically correct (13/30)
   - 6 marked as 100% AI
   - 7 marked as 100% human
   - 1 unable to be rated
   - If using "substantially correct" judgment: 23-57% accuracy range

**Key Finding**: "Turnitin was least effective in identifying texts that were hybrid."

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED (research findings)

**Robustness Study** (Machine-Humanized Text):

**Test Focus**: AI text modified by humanization tools

**Results**:
- **Unmodified AI detection**: 96% identified (at least partially)
- **Humanized AI detection**: Only 60% accuracy (near random)
- **Sensitivity/recall**: 21% for humanized text
- **F1 score**: 34% for humanized text

**Conclusion**: "Detection accuracy for machine-humanized AI-generated texts declined significantly"

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED (research findings)

**O-1 Model Study** (2025):

**Test Focus**: GPT-4o vs GPT-5 detection

**Results**:
- **False positives**: 1% (consistent with Turnitin claim)
- **False negatives**: Varied significantly by model version
- **GPT-5 text**: More likely to be misclassified as human-written than GPT-4o
- **Implication**: Newer AI models evade detection better

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED (research findings)

## 6. Known Limitations and Edge Cases

### 6.1 Official Limitations (DOCUMENTED)

**Source**: Turnitin guides and institutional policies

**Cannot Reliably Detect**:
1. Non-prose text (code, poetry, tables)
2. Short-form writing (<300 words)
3. Highly edited AI text
4. Newer AI model outputs (GPT-5+)
5. Prompt-engineered AI outputs
6. Recursively paraphrased text
7. Human-edited AI drafts

**Disproportionate Flagging Concerns** (DOCUMENTED):
Multiple institutions report concerns that AI detectors:
- Flag non-native English speakers at higher rates
- Flag neurodivergent students more frequently
- Flag formulaic/technical writing
- Flag assessment-specific language patterns

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED (limitations), DOCUMENTED (bias concerns)

## 8. Ethical Use Guidelines

### 8.1 Turnitin's Guidance (DOCUMENTED)

**Source**: Multiple official guides

**Recommended Practices**:
1. Never use as sole evidence of misconduct
2. Combine with other evidence:
   - False references/hallucinations
   - Difference from student's previous work
   - Language inconsistencies
   - Style inconsistencies
   - AI-specific caveats in text ("regenerate text")
   - File metadata anomalies
3. Speak with students about their process
4. Request drafts, notes, evidence of authorship
5. Discuss understanding of content
6. Consider institutional academic policies

**Evidence Classification**: DOCUMENTED

### 8.2 Institutional Guidelines (DOCUMENTED)

**University of Melbourne Guidance**:
- Focus on >20% scores only
- Use with entire work, not sentence-by-sentence
- Consider formulaic writing requirements
- Aware of student's typical expression style
- Second evidence piece required
- Not enough on its own for misconduct allegation

**Vanderbilt University Action** (2023):
- **Disabled Turnitin AI detector** institution-wide
- Reasons cited:
  - Less than 24-hour notice of feature launch
  - No option to disable initially
  - No transparency on how it works
  - False positive concerns (750 papers potentially mislabeled based on 1% rate)
  - Bias concerns (non-native speakers)
  - Privacy concerns with third-party detectors
  - Fundamental doubt if AI detection is solvable

## 11. Evaluation and Validation

### 11.1 Performance Metrics (INFERRED)

**Essential Measurements**:

1. **False Positive Rate (FPR)**
   - Percentage of human text incorrectly flagged
   - Critical metric for fairness
   - Target: <1% at document level (matching Turnitin claim)

2. **False Negative Rate (FNR)**
   - Percentage of AI text not detected
   - Important for effectiveness
   - Will vary by AI model tested

3. **Precision**
   - Of flagged text, how much is actually AI?
   - Positive Predictive Value

4. **Recall (Sensitivity)**
   - Of actual AI text, how much detected?
   - Critical for catching AI use

5. **F1 Score**
   - Harmonic mean of precision and recall
   - Overall model quality

6. **Model-Specific Performance**
   - Test against multiple AI models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4o, Claude, etc.)
   - Document per-model accuracy

7. **Modification Resilience**
   - Test against paraphrased AI text
   - Test against edited AI text
   - Test against humanized AI text

**Evidence Classification**: INFERRED (standard ML evaluation)

## 16. Conclusions

### 16.1 What We Know (DOCUMENTED)

1. **Technology Exists**: AI detection is possible with moderate accuracy
2. **Limitations Are Significant**: False positives, false negatives, bias concerns
3. **Not Definitive**: Probabilistic screening tool, not proof
4. **Requires Context**: Must be combined with human judgment
5. **Language-Specific**: English has best support
6. **Evolving Challenge**: Newer AI models harder to detect

### 16.2 What We Don't Know (UNAVAILABLE)

1. Turnitin's exact model architecture
2. Turnitin's training data and methodology
3. Turnitin's detection patterns and features
4. Turnitin's confidence calibration methods
5. Turnitin's model update frequency

### 16.3 What We Can Build

**Feasible**: Independent AI detection system using:
- Open-source models or commercial APIs
- Custom-trained classifier
- Transparent methodology
- Documented accuracy metrics
- Clear limitations
- Ethical safeguards

**Essential**: Must NOT claim equivalence to Turnitin. Must document our own accuracy, limitations, and biases.

### 16.4 Critical Recommendations

**For Our Implementation**:
1. **Transparency First**: Document everything about our model
2. **Measure Rigorously**: Test false positives/negatives extensively
3. **Disclose Clearly**: Users must know limitations
4. **Update Regularly**: AI landscape changes rapidly
5. **Emphasize Context**: Never present as definitive proof
6. **Provide Guidance**: Help educators use tool appropriately
7. **Consider Not Building**: AI detection is controversial and may not be worth the risk

**Alternative Approach**:
Consider focusing on similarity detection only and recommending assignment design changes over AI detection.
